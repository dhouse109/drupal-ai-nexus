# CTA Copy Guidelines

This document captures the CTA language patterns approved through the staged CTA refresh.

The goal is to keep future edits consistent across pages, especially when new sections are added quickly through AI-assisted design or visual editing.

## Core principle
A CTA should feel like a clear next step, not an intake form.

Good CTA language on 109.studio should:
- sound principal-led, calm, and direct
- lower friction for visitors who may not know Damien yet
- name the destination in plain language
- feel commercially useful without sounding salesy

CTA language should avoid:
- procedural intake phrasing
- asking the visitor to self-diagnose too early
- generic brochure labels
- repeated use of `Review offerings`
- high-pressure or overly evaluative phrasing

## Primary CTA pattern
Use the primary CTA for the most human and direct next step.

### Default primary CTA
`Start a conversation`

Use this when the page is still early in the trust-building process or when the user has not yet seen enough proof to warrant a more specific ask.

### Approved context-specific primary CTAs
`Start with your situation`

Use this after proof or background when the visitor likely needs help deciding which engagement fits.

`Talk through a similar challenge`

Use this after essays, insights, or thought leadership when the visitor may recognize their own situation in the material.

## Secondary CTA pattern
The secondary CTA should usually name the destination rather than describe a browsing action.

### Approved secondary CTAs
`See how 109 helps`

Use this when the destination is the broader practice or services overview.

`See starting points`

Use this when the destination is the offerings page or when the visitor needs help choosing a shaped entry path.

`Explore applied AI`

Use this when the destination is the applied AI overview page.

`See AI in practice`

Use this when the destination is the examples, narratives, or applied AI proof page.

## CTA usage by page type
### Early trust pages
Examples:
- homepage hero
- global footer
- contact-adjacent invitations

Preferred pattern:
- primary: `Start a conversation`
- secondary: `See how 109 helps` or `See starting points`

### Offer-selection pages
Examples:
- Services
- Offerings

Preferred pattern:
- primary: `Start a conversation` or `Start with your situation`
- secondary: `See starting points`

### Proof-led pages
Examples:
- About
- Insights
- AI in Practice

Preferred pattern:
- primary can be more specific to the proof just shown
- secondary should usually remain `See starting points`

## Supporting copy guidance
The sentence under a CTA should usually do one of these jobs:
1. reduce pressure
2. explain what happens next
3. clarify why this path is useful

Strong examples:
- `A short note is enough.`
- `Damien reviews each inquiry personally.`
- `The next step is usually a short conversation or a clear recommendation on where to begin.`
- `You do not need the full scope figured out yet.`

## Phrases to avoid unless there is a strong reason
Avoid these by default:
- `Review offerings`
- `Tell 109 what you are working on`
- `Start with a short note about the product, platform, or technical decision in front of you`
- `The clearest path through the site is simple`
- `30-minute conversation`

These phrases are not wrong in every context, but they tend to feel more procedural, more rigid, or less introductory than the approved system.

## Maintenance note
When adding a new section with CTAs, start by choosing:
- whether the page is early-trust, offer-selection, or proof-led
- which approved primary CTA best matches that trust level
- which destination the secondary CTA should name directly

If the copy starts to sound like a form, a brochure, or a process diagram, simplify it.
